These are storage divisions, not chapter boundaries. The Buster narrative runs mainly through `rom/00–05`; the Vandel narrative begins near the end of `rom/0F` and continues through `rom/12`. Optional events and reusable dialogue follow each main sequence. The stories share characters and incidents but lead to opposing outcomes: do not combine their endings into one chronology. **Buster story, chapter 1: returning to an occupied homeland**

The player’s Buster, `{hero}`, returns to Forjemini Island, commonly called the Twin Islands, with an elderly Appraiser. The two mirrored islands once prospered through silverwork and agriculture, protected by silver goddesses and ancient rings. Vandels destroyed West Village and built a Golden Castle.

As a child, the hero escaped an attacking Vandel because his father disguised the escape as a race. His father died protecting him. The hero wears the cloth his father clutched as a bandanna and has trained for revenge. The Appraiser also mourns someone: his pendant belonged to the island’s murdered Appraiser, an old friend. These are separate bereavements.

After monsters wreck their ship, the hero reaches East Village. Lena fears for Zena, her younger twin sister; their uncanny emotional connection proves meaningful. Zena is rescued outside and becomes a roaming merchant/healer. Lena gives the hero a handmade Silver Charm. Both sisters show affection for him, but neither relationship is formally resolved.

Beet, Poala and Kissu arrive as outside allies. Beet sleeps at the hero’s home; Poala is practical and sharp with him, while Kissu jokes about their “married-couple quarrels.” This is teasing, not proof they are married. Kissu hints at a troubled past without explaining it here. The hero’s mother accepts his determination while offering a home to return to.

**Chapter 2: revenge becomes responsibility**

Assignments introduce beach monsters, coin-eating Kanecks, weapons, Divine Attacks and Saiga. Harden appears in villain interludes: a six-star tyrant obsessed with gold, offended by silver and convinced of his own beauty.

A dying messenger brings news that Mirei Forest Village has fallen. Its chief was the East Village Chief’s younger brother. The seals’ destruction turns their surroundings gold and accompanies increased monsters and abnormal weather. Horey supplies Holy Silver; Lena’s Sacred Flame melts it inside her silver lamp, creating Holy Silver Drops. One dose repairs one ring. Restored rings also reveal routes home.

Lena initially rejects the elders’ insistence that the hero must bear the danger. Her distress is not simple timidity: she objects to everyone depending on one young person. The Chief is grieving and conflicted, not casually indifferent. Eventually Lena recognizes that her prayers and the hero’s actions both matter.

The hero repairs nine eastern rings, rescues Beet’s party at a sabotaged bridge and can recruit them. Milfa investigates the seals and supports the newcomer. Optional companions have their own motives: Grest and Badu are reformed rogues, Zeke chases women, and Goran/Shiba demand payment.

**Chapter 3: western resistance and the twins**

Zena’s bravado collapses when she faces a Vandel; afterward she jokes before her shaking legs betray her terror. The Resistance consists of survivors of destroyed villages. Muterin’s survivors are being worked to death mining gold. Three western hideouts provide support. Ark’s party, dispatched from Bekatorte by Melmade, investigates effects spreading beyond the islands.

Lena tries to contribute directly by fetching silver from Horey and is attacked by Baleas. Zena senses her danger. Keep this rescue separate from Lena’s later abduction.

Shagie draws Harden’s attention to the troublesome sisters. A false Zena lures Lena out; Orozon’s sleep powers disable the village, even its living gate. The real Zena later notices Lena’s cracked charm. She has not betrayed her sister: the imposture must remain distinct from her genuine speech and loyalty.

After Orozon’s defeat, the heroes face an unusually powerful Vandel who withdraws. The group suspects a connection with the Golden Castle and the hero’s revenge, but certainty comes later. Beet’s party helps free the miners while the hero pursues Lena. The enemy destroys the castle’s only approach bridge; the Bridge Master rebuilds it under guard.

**Chapter 4: the true enemy**

Inside the castle, Lena is bait for a trap using Vandel effigies. Familiar villains such as Grineed, Ventura, Frausky and Rozgoat appear as magical reproductions, not evidence that their original selves survived. Poala stays with rescued Lena while Beet and Kissu accompany the hero.

Kissu notices that `{vandel}`’s emblem matches the hero’s bandanna. This identifies the long-sought enemy: the player-created Vandel, not simply Harden because he owns the castle. Do not make earlier suspicions sound conclusive.

After losing a battle, Harden retreats. His subordinate then strikes him down and reveals overwhelming power. The hero confronts `{vandel}` and remembers his father’s wishes that he grow strong **and kind**. The victory ends with the formerly withdrawn hero laughing with his friends. There is no explicit mercy ending here.

A separate failure outcome describes every ring and goddess destroyed, the islands transformed into a Vandel-controlled realm of death. It is not the sequel to victory.

**Vandel story, chapters 1–2: conquest and apprenticeship**

The Vandel route begins earlier, with `{vandel}` as Harden’s one-star subordinate. Harden teaches that a Vandel’s real rivals are other Vandels—a principle that anticipates his own overthrow. The player also chooses an epithet.

Destroying West Village’s goddess earns the second star. Shagie explains that ancient inhabitants sealed extensive gold deposits beneath the islands. More importantly, dark-energy veins intertwine with the gold. `{vandel}` identified this opportunity and recruited Harden’s involvement: Harden wants wealth; his subordinate wants the buried power. Shagie knows and encourages that hidden ambition.

Castle construction requires Build Ants, distinct from Gourmet Ants. Harden borrows recklessly from Ganeer and shifts the consequences onto his subordinate. The conquest of the mountain village establishes the forced-labor mine. Yagil first collects debts, then seeks refuge in the player’s army after witnessing their strength against Milfa. Ganeer’s collection events contain payment and combat branches.

**Chapters 3–4: defeating resistance**

Shagie offers stars for finding, destroying and reporting each island’s nine rings. Other Vandels challenge or join the rising player. Harden sends them into an ambush by Baleas and Elder, then claims the betrayal was intended to cultivate hatred. His explanation is self-serving, not a confirmed benevolent motive.

The player attacks Resistance fighters and encounters Zena and the Buster hero. At Mirei, the chief provides a false location for the repair item after a defender is attacked. The invaders kill despite his apparent cooperation; the chest is empty. This is the same doomed chief mourned in the Buster route.

Shagie identifies the sisters’ importance. A proposed infiltration uses a Droman disguised as the Buster and Orozon’s mental attacks; the Buster-route abduction instead shows a false Zena. Preserve the route-specific wording rather than silently making both scenes identical. Slade rescues Zena during the confrontation. Torture reveals that a Sacred Flame is required for repairs, but not its location.

**Chapter 5: supremacy and alternate outcomes**

Destroying eighteen field rings does not finish the seal: East Village’s goddess remains. The player breaks through Resistance positions, Ark’s party, militia and finally the hero. His dying confrontation and father’s flashback confirm the revenge connection from the opposing perspective. Shagie awards the seventh star after the final conquest, then urges a battle with Beet’s party and eventual elevation to the Eight Shining Stars.

Harden can be challenged and killed; Shagie recognizes the player as castle lord and grants money. Later conversations explicitly acknowledge Harden’s death. Conversely, restoration of every ring produces a Vandel retreat ending. These are branching event blocks, not a single mandatory sequence proved by ROM order. No supplied dialogue establishes that the player actually becomes one of the Eight Shining Stars.
